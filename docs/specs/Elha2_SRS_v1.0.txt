1. Introduction

1.1     Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) specifies the software requirements of Elha2, re-
lease 1.0: an Arabic-first, AI-assisted mobile app and web portal that reduces food waste in Egypt.
It covers the whole product, not a single subsystem: the household mobile app, the business, NGO,
administrator and government web portal, and the backend services they share.

    The SRS states what Elha2 shall do and how well. How it is built is described in the companion
Software Design Document (SDD) [3]. This version is the Milestone 1 baseline of the course project.
It contains the full product backlog and marks the subset that we will implement during the course
(Section 6.6). It is a living document: we update it at the end of every sprint and record each
change in the revision history.


1.2     Document Overview
Chapter 1 introduces the product and this document. Chapter 2 describes the product as a whole:
its context, functions, users and stakeholders. Chapter 3 specifies the interfaces to users, hardware
and other software. Chapter 4 holds the product backlog, organised by feature. Chapter 5 gives
the non-functional requirements and business rules. Chapter 6 covers database, localisation and
legal requirements, the global and societal considerations, the risks, the features we will implement
during the course, and the conclusion. The appendices hold the glossary, the use case model, the
open-item list and the verification matrices.


1.3     Product Scope
Elha2 (Egyptian Arabic for “catch it!”) is an Arabic-first, AI-assisted mobile app for Android and
iOS that reduces food waste in Egypt at three points: Prevent, Sell, Donate. A web portal
serves food businesses, NGOs, platform administrators and government partners.

 Prevent (households). A smart pantry fills itself from receipts, online orders, photos and voice
  notes. AI estimates when each item will spoil, sends reminders, and suggests Egyptian recipes
  that use food before it goes off.

 Sell (businesses). Restaurants, bakeries, hotels and supermarkets list surplus as surprise bags
  or specific items at 50–70% off, with a pickup window.

 Donate (NGOs). Listings that are not sold or collected pass automatically to a partner NGO;
  households can also donate sealed pantry items or buy a bag for an NGO.
Software Requirements Specification for Elha2                                                  Page 2


 Measure (everyone). Every confirmed action is converted into kilograms of food saved, Egyp-
  tian pounds saved, CO2 e avoided and meals provided, at personal, business, neighbourhood and
  national level.

1.3.1       Motivation and Benefits
Egyptian households waste about 18 million tonnes of food a year, roughly 163 kg per person
against a 79 kg global average, and households produce 60% of all food waste worldwide [4], yet
every food-saving app active in Egypt and the Gulf targets business surplus only.

    Households are under-served for a simple reason: tracking food at home is tedious, so people
stop. Elha2’s motivation is therefore twofold. It addresses the largest and most neglected source
of waste, and it uses existing AI models to remove the manual effort that makes household tools
fail. The benefits are lower grocery bills for households, recovered revenue and lower disposal costs
for businesses, a steady flow of safe food for NGOs, and credible data for national reporting. The
overall goal is SDG Target 12.3: halving per-capita food waste at retail and consumer level by
2030 [5].

1.3.2       Mission Statement

         To help Egyptian households and food businesses catch food before it goes to waste,
                    and to turn what remains into affordable meals and donations.

    Every feature is judged against this mission: a requirement that does not serve it is out of
scope.

1.3.3       Out of Scope
The first release covers Greater Cairo (Cairo and Giza), in Arabic and English. Elha2 does not
run its own delivery fleet: all orders are pickup, and NGOs collect donations themselves or through
partner volunteers. Medical or nutritional advice, alcohol, home-cooked food and person-to-person
food sharing are out of scope.

2. Overall Description

2.1      Product Perspective
Elha2 is a new, self-contained product. It sits inside a wider food-saving ecosystem: households and
businesses produce surplus, NGOs absorb it, and external services provide login, payments, maps,
notifications and AI. Figure 2.1 shows this system boundary as a context diagram; the interfaces
are specified in Chapter 3.

    The mobile app (Flutter) and the web portal (React) share one backend API (NestJS), which
stores data in PostgreSQL with PostGIS (hosted on Supabase) and runs a scheduler for reminders
and donation routing. All AI calls pass through a single AI gateway in the backend, except text and
barcode recognition, which run on the phone. The architecture, with UML component diagrams,
is in the SDD [3]. Figure 2.2 shows how food enters the household pantry without manual typing.

3. External Interface Requirements

3.1     User Interfaces
Elha2 has two user interfaces: the mobile app and the web portal.

 Mobile app. Five main tabs in a bottom navigation bar: Home, Pantry, Deals, Donate and
  Impact, plus a profile and settings page. The Pantry tab has one large “Add food” button that
  opens the five capture methods (receipt, barcode, photo, voice, manual).

 Web portal. Responsive layout for desktop and tablet, with separate areas for businesses,
  NGOs, administrators and government analysts, chosen by the signed-in user’s role. The counter-
  staff pickup view also works in a phone browser.

 Style guides. Material Design 3 on Android and Apple’s Human Interface Guidelines on
  iOS [10]; one Elha2 colour palette and type scale shared by app and portal.

 Language and layout. Arabic by default with a full right-to-left layout; English with left-to-
  right layout; switchable at any time (US-1.02, NFR-16).

 Accessibility. Touch targets of at least 48 dp; system text scaling up to 200%; screen-reader
  labels on every control; colour is never the only signal (NFR-17). Every main action has an icon
  and a text label (NFR-18).

4.9       E9 Impact Dashboard and Reporting

4.9.1     Description and Priority
Shows every user type the impact of what it saves, in kilograms, Egyptian pounds, CO2 e and meals,
with a published method. Its design rests on three rules. First, only verified actions count: a
pickup confirmed by code, a donation confirmed by the NGO, or a pantry item marked as used.
Second, every number has a published method: each food category carries a weight, a CO2 e
factor and an average price, stored in a versioned table that admins update from cited sources.
Third, each user type sees what helps them decide, from a household’s weekly waste rate to
a ministry’s district-level totals.

     For each verified item i with weight wi (kg) in food category c(i):
                       X                                        X
     CO2 e avoided =        wi × EFc(i)       Money saved =           (original pricei − price paidi )      (4.1)
                        i                                         i

where EFc is the emission factor (kg CO2 e per kg of food) for category c. Meals provided use a
single documented conversion from kilograms, shown on the “How we calculate” screen.

6.4.1    Trade-offs and Possible Negative Effects
 Impact data versus privacy. Richer data would make national statistics more precise, but
  could identify households. We publish only aggregates of at least 10 users (NFR-15).

 Automation versus accuracy. AI removes typing but can be wrong. Every AI result is
  reviewed by the user before it is saved, and the user can always enter items by hand (NFR-22,
  NFR-30).

 Digital divide. Elha2 needs a smartphone, which excludes some of the people most affected by
  food insecurity. NGO routing reaches them through organisations rather than through the app.

 Discounts versus donations. Selling surplus could reduce what reaches charities. Auto-
